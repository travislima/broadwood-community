Where we stand

48 of 800 households
Members today Goal: every property in Broadwood & Charlo

48 households currently contribute R100 a month — that's what funds the 15 cameras and their monitoring. There are about 800 properties in Broadwood and Charlo. More members simply means more cameras, faster response and better-kept public spaces.

What your R100 pays for

The association is run by volunteers who live here. Membership money goes into the neighbourhood, not into salaries.

Camera network

15 security cameras already watch over our streets, professionally monitored 24/7. More members means more cameras covering more corners.

Crime prevention

We work hand-in-hand with the SAPS, security companies and neighbourhood watch — helping the role players prevent and fight crime in our area.

Parks & green spaces

Cutting back trees, clearing overgrown high-risk spots, and helping care for the parks and green spaces our kids grow up playing in.

A connected community

Active WhatsApp groups for emergencies and everyday neighbourhood life — over 900 residents already look out for one another.

Frequently asked questions

Something we haven't covered? Ask on the social WhatsApp group.

What does my R100 a month actually pay for?

Camera hardware and maintenance, 24/7 professional monitoring, clearing of high-risk overgrown areas, tree cutting, and supporting the people who prevent and fight crime in our area. The association is run by volunteers — your money funds the work, not salaries.

Who runs the association?

Your neighbours. The Broadwood/Charlo Residents Association is a volunteer association for the Broadwood and Charlo suburbs of Gqeberha (Port Elizabeth), run by people who live here and want the area to thrive.

I rent my home — can I still join?

Yes. Safety and a well-kept neighbourhood benefit everyone who lives here, owner or tenant. Every household counts toward the goal.

I can't commit to a monthly debit order. Can I still help?

Yes — once-off donations of any amount are welcome and appreciated. See the banking details in the membership section above. You can also help simply by being active on the emergency group and looking out for your neighbours.

Which WhatsApp group should I join?

Both! The Emergency group is strictly for urgent alerts — keep notifications on. The Social group is for everything else: municipal updates, questions, recommendations and community chatter.

Only 48 out of 800? Why should I bother?

Because those 48 households already fund 15 monitored cameras — with just 6% of the neighbourhood on board. That's the whole point: small, shared contributions go a long way. Every new member makes the next camera and the next clean-up possible.

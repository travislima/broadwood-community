Membership at work

What your R100 pays for

The association is run by volunteers who live here — run by neighbours, spent on the neighbourhood. Membership money goes into the work, not into salaries.

Camera network

15 security cameras already watch over our streets — actively monitored, with professional support sponsored by CityWide Security — and six more are being installed. More members means more cameras, placed where they count — entry and exit points first, then proven hotspots.

Parks & green spaces

Cutting back trees, clearing overgrown high-risk spots, and helping care for the parks and green spaces our kids grow up playing in.

Crime prevention

We work hand-in-hand with the SAPS, security companies and neighbourhood watch — helping the role players prevent and fight crime in our area.

A connected community

Active WhatsApp groups for emergencies and everyday neighbourhood life — over 900 residents already look out for one another.

Frequently asked questions

Something we haven't covered?

Ask on the social WhatsApp group.

What does my R100 a month actually pay for?

Camera hardware and maintenance, active camera monitoring, clearing of high-risk overgrown areas, tree cutting, and supporting the people who prevent and fight crime in our area. The association is run by volunteers — your money funds the work, not salaries.

How do I know the money is well spent?

The association is run by volunteer neighbours, and the work is visible: cameras go up on our streets, high-risk spots get cleared, and updates are shared on the Facebook page and community groups — so you can see your contribution at work.

Update schedule to be confirmed with the committee.

What's the goal, and what's the plan?

The camera rollout follows a structured, security-first plan. Installation starts on the neighbourhood's perimeter — the known entry and exit points used for criminal activity — because controlling access routes is the most effective way to identify, track and deter offenders. In parallel, cameras go up in crime hotspots identified from actual incident reports and observed trends, not convenience or preference.

The broader goal is progressive coverage across the majority of streets, so we can monitor movement patterns, link incidents, and strip away the anonymity criminals rely on. It's a targeted strategy to disrupt criminal activity and improve response — not blanket surveillance for minor issues. Updates are shared as installations progress.

Who runs the association?

Your neighbours. The Broadwood Charlo Residents Association is a volunteer association for the Broadwood and Charlo suburbs of Gqeberha (Port Elizabeth), run by people who live here and want the area to thrive.

I rent my home — can I still join?

Yes. Safety and a well-kept neighbourhood benefit everyone who lives here, owner or tenant. Every household counts toward the goal.

I can't commit to a monthly debit order. Can I still help?

Yes — once-off donations of any amount are welcome and appreciated. See the banking details in the membership section above. You can also help simply by being active on the emergency group and looking out for your neighbours — and volunteers are always needed for the fundraisers and clean-ups we organise.

Which WhatsApp group should I join?

All three! The Emergency group is strictly for immediate alerts where assistance is required from the NHW, security companies, SAPS or EMS services — keep notifications on. The Broadwood Security Discussion group is for general security discussions. The Social group is for everything else: municipal updates, questions, recommendations and community chatter.

Only 48 out of 800? Why should I bother?

Because those 48 households already fund 15 monitored cameras, with another six being installed — and that's with just 6% of the neighbourhood on board. That's the whole point: small, shared contributions go a long way. Every new member makes the next camera and the next clean-up possible.
